# Chamberly Customer Interview Guide

**Purpose:** Guide for interviewing independent doctors and chamber operators in Bangladesh to validate Chamberly's assumptions and product direction.
**Last Updated:** 2025-01-10
**Target Respondents:** Independent doctors and chamber operators in Bangladesh

---

## Interview Context

**Important Principles:**
- Ask about real past behavior and actual problems, not leading questions about Chamberly
- Do not mention Chamberly's proposed features unless asking for feedback on a specific concept
- Focus on understanding current workflows, pain points, and willingness to pay
- Record the location, chamber type, and practice size for each interview
- Avoid assuming that competitor features represent customer demand

---

## Section 1: Introduction and Context

**Opening:**
"Thank you for taking the time to speak with me. I'm researching how independent doctors in Bangladesh manage their chamber operations. I'm interested in understanding your current workflow, the challenges you face, and what would make your practice more efficient."

**Context Questions:**
1. How long have you been practicing independently?
2. How many chambers do you currently operate? (locations, days per week)
3. How many patients do you typically see per day?
4. What type of practice do you have? (general practice, specialist, etc.)
5. Do you have staff (receptionist, assistant)? If so, how many?

---

## Section 2: Current Appointment and Serial Management

**Goal:** Understand how doctors currently manage appointments and queues.

**Questions:**
1. How do patients currently book appointments with you? (phone calls, WhatsApp, walk-in only, online booking, etc.)
2. How do you track who is next in the queue? (paper register, verbal, digital system)
3. What happens when a patient arrives without an appointment? (walk-in process)
4. How do you handle patients who miss their appointments or arrive late?
5. How do receptionists or assistants manage the queue when you're with a patient?
6. What problems do you encounter with the current appointment/queue system?
7. How do patients find out their position in the queue or estimated wait time?

**Follow-up Probes:**
- "Can you walk me through a typical day from the first patient to the last?"
- "What happens when two receptionists are managing the queue simultaneously?"
- "How do you handle serial number allocation across multiple chambers?"

---

## Section 3: Multi-Chamber Scheduling

**Goal:** Validate multi-chamber prevalence and complexity.

**Questions:**
1. Do you operate multiple chambers in different locations? If yes, how many?
2. How do you manage schedules across different chambers? (separate registers, one system, mental tracking)
3. Do different chambers have different fee structures? (new vs. follow-up, different locations)
4. How do you track revenue per chamber?
5. How do you avoid schedule conflicts between chambers?
6. What is the biggest challenge with managing multiple chambers?
7. Do you have staff at each chamber, or do they move between locations?

**Follow-up Probes:**
- "How do you ensure you don't double-book yourself across chambers?"
- "What happens when a chamber is closed or you're running late?"

---

## Section 4: Staff and Receptionist Workflows

**Goal:** Understand staff roles, permissions, and workflows.

**Questions:**
1. What tasks do your receptionists or assistants handle? (appointments, queue, payments, patient registration)
2. How do they access patient information or appointment schedules? (shared login, separate system, verbal communication)
3. What information should they NOT have access to? (prescriptions, financial reports, patient history)
4. How do they handle patient check-ins and queue management?
5. What problems do they face with their current tools or permissions?
6. How do you coordinate with staff across multiple chambers?

**Follow-up Probes:**
- "What happens when a receptionist needs to reschedule an appointment?"
- "How do they handle payment collection without seeing prescriptions?"

---

## Section 5: Appointment and Operational Problems

**Goal:** Identify real pain points with current methods.

**Questions:**
1. What are the most common problems you face with appointment management?
2. How often do patients miss their appointments? (estimate percentage)
3. What is the impact of missed appointments on your practice? (revenue loss, schedule disruption)
4. How do you handle cancellations or rescheduling?
5. What problems do you encounter with serial number allocation or queue management?
6. How do patients react when they don't know their queue position or wait time?
7. What is the most time-consuming part of your daily administrative work?

**Follow-up Probes:**
- "Can you give me a specific example of when a missed appointment caused a problem?"
- "What is the most frustrating part of managing the queue for you or your staff?"

---

## Section 6: Software and Tools

**Goal:** Understand current technology usage and spending.

**Questions:**
1. Do you currently use any software for your practice? (if yes, which one, how long, pros/cons)
2. If you don't use software, what tools do you use? (paper register, Excel, WhatsApp, etc.)
3. How much do you spend monthly on software or administrative tools?
4. Have you tried other chamber management software? (if yes, which ones, why did you stop using them?)
5. What would make you switch from your current method to a new software?
6. What would make you NOT switch from your current method?

**Follow-up Probes:**
- "What is the one thing your current software (or method) does really well?"
- "What is the one thing you wish it did differently?"

---

## Section 7: Willingness to Pay and Value

**Goal:** Understand pricing sensitivity and perceived value.

**Questions:**
1. If a software could save you 30 minutes of administrative work per day, how much would that be worth to you per month?
2. What would you expect to pay for a software that manages appointments, patients, and prescriptions? (monthly range)
3. Would you prefer a monthly subscription or a yearly payment with a discount?
4. What features would make you willing to pay more? (e.g., SMS reminders, online booking, advanced reports)
5. What would make you unwilling to pay even a small amount? (e.g., complex setup, unreliable system, poor support)
6. How do you typically evaluate whether to purchase new software for your practice?

**Follow-up Probes:**
- "If I showed you a tool that costs ৳700/month but saves you 30 minutes/day, would you try it?"
- "What would you need to see in a demo to consider switching from your current method?"

---

## Section 8: Language and Interface Preferences

**Goal:** Understand Bangla vs. English preference for daily operations.

**Questions:**
1. What language do you use for your daily patient interactions? (Bangla, English, both)
2. What language would you prefer for a software interface? (Bangla-first, English-first, both)
3. Would your staff prefer Bangla or English?
4. Would your patients benefit from a Bangla interface for booking or viewing prescriptions?

**Follow-up Probes:**
- "If the software was in Bangla, would that affect your willingness to use it?"
- "Would you need both languages available, or is one sufficient?"

---

## Section 9: SMS Reminders and Online Booking

**Goal:** Validate demand for SMS reminders and online booking (without over-selling).

**Questions:**
1. Do you currently send appointment reminders to patients? (how: phone, WhatsApp, SMS, manual)
2. How often do patients miss appointments because they forgot?
3. Would SMS reminders reduce your no-show rate? (why/why not)
4. Do patients ever ask to book appointments online or by WhatsApp?
5. Would online booking reduce your phone call volume?
6. Would you be willing to pay for SMS reminders? (if yes, how much per message or monthly)

**Follow-up Probes:**
- "What would you need to see to trust an online booking system?"
- "Are there any concerns about patients booking online (e.g., reliability, accuracy)?"

---

## Section 10: Prescription and Patient Records

**Goal:** Understand needs and concerns about digital prescriptions and patient data (without assuming implementation).

**Questions:**
1. How do you currently write prescriptions? (handwritten, digital, template-based)
2. What are the main problems with your current prescription method? (legibility, time, record-keeping)
3. How do you maintain patient records? (paper files, digital system, memory)
4. What concerns would you have about storing patient data in a cloud system?
5. Are there any regulatory or compliance requirements you're aware of for digital prescriptions in Bangladesh?
6. Would you need digital prescriptions immediately, or is that a lower priority?

**Follow-up Probes:**
- "What would need to be in place for you to trust a digital prescription system?"
- "Are there specific types of patient information you would NOT want stored digitally?"

---

## Section 11: Closing and Next Steps

**Closing:**
"Thank you for sharing your time and insights. This information will help me design a chamber management tool that actually addresses real needs."

**Closing Questions:**
1. If you could change one thing about your current appointment/queue management, what would it be?
2. Is there anything else about your daily workflow that I should have asked about?
3. Would you be willing to test a prototype of a new tool if I build one?
4. Can I contact you again if I have follow-up questions or want to show you a prototype?

---

## Interview Best Practices

**Do:**
- Record audio (with permission) or take detailed notes
- Ask follow-up questions to dig deeper into interesting responses
- Observe the actual environment if possible (register, queue area)
- Ask for specific examples ("Tell me about a time when...")
- Validate assumptions with multiple doctors

**Don't:**
- Lead the respondent toward Chamberly's proposed features
- Assume that a competitor feature is necessary just because it exists
- Make promises about what Chamberly will build
- Ask about sensitive patient information or real clinical data
- Push for a purchase decision during the interview

---

## Data to Record for Each Interview

- **Respondent ID:** (anonymous identifier)
- **Location:** (city, area)
- **Practice Type:** (general practice, specialist, etc.)
- **Chambers:** (number and locations)
- **Daily Patient Volume:** (estimated range)
- **Staff Count:** (receptionists, assistants)
- **Current Software:** (name, duration, pros/cons)
- **Monthly Software Spend:** (amount)
- **Key Pain Points:** (3-5 specific problems)
- **Willingness to Pay:** (price range, reasoning)
- **Language Preference:** (Bangla, English, both)
- **SMS/Online Booking Interest:** (interest level, concerns)
- **Prescription/Patient Record Needs:** (urgency, concerns)

## Synthesis Guide

After completing 10-20 interviews, synthesize findings:

1. **Common Pain Points:** What problems do 70%+ of doctors mention?
2. **Multi-Chamber Prevalence:** What percentage actually work across multiple chambers?
3. **Staff Workflow:** What are the common staff roles and permission needs?
4. **Pricing Sensitivity:** What price range seems acceptable? What features justify higher pricing?
5. **Language Preference:** Is Bangla-first actually preferred, or is English acceptable?
6. **SMS/Online Booking:** Is there genuine demand, or is it a nice-to-have?
7. **Prescription Urgency:** Is digital prescription a top priority or lower priority?
8. **Competitor Usage:** What software are they currently using? Why do they stay or leave?

**Decision Framework:**
- If 70%+ mention a problem, it's a validated pain point
- If 50%+ say they would pay at price X, pricing is validated
- If a competitor feature is mentioned as "essential" by 50%+, it's validated demand
- If a competitor feature is never mentioned spontaneously, it may not be essential

**Avoid:**
- Assuming that a feature mentioned by one doctor is a validated market need
- Assuming that competitor pricing is the "right" pricing
- Assuming that complex features (AI, video consultation) are necessary
- Assuming that free tiers will drive adoption without validating value
